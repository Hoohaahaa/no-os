# Vision

## Long-term outcome

Create a dependable operating and intelligence layer for a portfolio of independent products. It should coordinate infrastructure and development capabilities, make product creation repeatable, provide AI-assisted research and execution, and preserve useful knowledge across the portfolio.

## Outcomes that matter

1. **Shorter path from idea to working product.** Reuse setup, deployment, data, identity, observability, and workflow capabilities where appropriate.
2. **Lower repeated engineering effort.** Discover and reuse existing solutions instead of rebuilding them.
3. **Better technical decisions.** Compare providers and architectures using evidence, cost, risk, reversibility, and product context.
4. **Operational clarity.** Know what exists, who owns it, how it is connected, what it costs, and whether it is healthy.
5. **Safe collaboration between products.** Share explicitly selected services, events, and knowledge while keeping customer data and business logic isolated.
6. **Compounding knowledge.** Turn experiments, incidents, decisions, and outcomes into reusable learning.
7. **Controlled autonomy.** Delegate bounded tasks to specialized agents while retaining human control over consequential actions.

## Near-term focus

The immediate priority is not to build all four phases at once. It is to establish a coherent foundation and then prove one complete, useful vertical slice: register a project, connect a provider with least privilege, inspect its capabilities, and report a truthful status.

## Boundaries

- Products remain independently deployable and replaceable.
- Shared infrastructure is optional, not mandatory.
- No provider is assumed to be universally best.
- No free tier is assumed to be permanent or sufficient for commercial production.
- AI output is not treated as verified fact until checked.
- Sensitive data is not copied into central knowledge by default.
- Automation is introduced only when it is safer and more reliable than the manual process it replaces.

## How to measure progress

Measure lead time to provision a new product, repeated setup steps eliminated, integration reliability, incident recovery time, cost visibility, permission scope, test coverage of critical workflows, and the rate at which documented learning changes later decisions. Avoid vanity metrics.
